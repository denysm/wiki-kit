# Methods for barriers, experiments and cases

Three classic lean methods, adapted to personal development. Use one only when
it fits the request; the five points of the challenge stay the main contract.

## Five whys: a barrier that repeats

1. State the symptom as a fact from the wiki, with a date ("passed over in the
   last two review cycles").
2. Ask why and answer from evidence: the owner's words, notes, the goals log.
   A guess is labelled as a hypothesis.
3. Repeat until the answer is a cause the owner can change. Five is a habit,
   not a rule.
4. "Not enough discipline", "bad luck" or "my manager" are not root causes: ask
   what made them possible or decisive.
5. Check backwards: if this cause were removed, would the symptom go? If the
   answers branch, follow each branch briefly.
6. End with one change and how to see within a month whether it worked.

## Experiment: a bold option that is uncertain (plan, do, check, act)

- **Plan**: the hypothesis, today's baseline, a measurable success criterion
  and a check date.
- **Do**: the smallest version, time-boxed and reversible; note what deviated.
- **Check**: compare with the baseline on the check date, not by feeling.
- **Act**: keep it and make it a habit, adjust and run again, or drop it. After
  three failed rounds, go back to the five whys.

## A3: a case on one page

For a promotion or pay review, a role change or a move: one page the owner can
hand to whoever decides.

1. Background: why this matters now.
2. Current state: role, scope and results, with numbers from the wiki.
3. Target: the level, role or figure asked for, and by when.
4. Gap and causes: what stands between 2 and 3 (five whys if needed).
5. Countermeasures: what the owner offers or will do.
6. Plan: who does what, by when.
7. Follow-up: when and how to check.

Every number traces to the wiki; a missing one is marked as a gap, never
estimated. Market figures need a source and a date. Rehearsing the
conversation itself is the job of `conversation-prep`.

Sources: five whys, PDCA and A3 are standard methods of lean management (Toyota
Production System); this text is the kit's own.
